## Named for What Blooms Here

Azalea, Oregon, is named for the wild azaleas that bloom in the surrounding hills every spring — and that's about as complicated as the town gets.

This unincorporated community at I-5 Exit 88 sits at 1,657 feet in Douglas County, roughly 10 miles northeast of Glendale, in the forested mountain country between the Rogue Valley and the Umpqua Valley. There is no downtown. The old Azalea-Glen Road — a remnant of the Pacific Highway that preceded I-5 — passes through what remains of the original settlement: a few residences, the memory of a general store that once served highway travelers, and the kind of quiet that comes from being a place most people drive past at 70 miles per hour.

But Azalea has a secret the locals prefer to keep to themselves.

## The Lake

[Galesville Reservoir](https://www.southernoregon.org/cities/azalea/), accessible from Exit 88 via Upper Cow Creek Road, is a 640-acre lake tucked into the hills east of town. The reservoir — built by the Army Corps of Engineers on Cow Creek — offers fishing for bass, trout, and bluegill, with a boat ramp, picnic areas, and seasonal camping. The locals call it simply "the lake."

Galesville doesn't appear on most tourist itineraries, which is exactly how the people who fish it want things to stay. It's a calm, uncrowded body of water surrounded by forest, with none of the development or crowds that characterize more popular Oregon lakes. For travelers willing to take the exit and drive 15 minutes into the hills, it's one of the more pleasant surprises on the I-5 corridor.

## Between Two Valleys

Azalea's geographic significance is the transition it marks. South of here, the terrain belongs to the Rogue Valley — open, agricultural, Mediterranean-warm. North of here, the Umpqua Valley begins to assert itself — greener, cooler, more forested. Azalea sits in the canyon country between the two, at one of the higher elevations on this stretch of I-5.

The surrounding Douglas County backcountry offers forest roads, seasonal wildflower displays, and the kind of solitude that Oregon's more famous destinations can't provide. It's not a destination town. It's a landscape town — a place where the reason to stop is the country itself.

## Getting to Azalea

**I-5 Exit 88** provides access to Azalea and the road to Galesville Reservoir. Follow Upper Cow Creek Road east from the exit for reservoir access.

Azalea is 30 miles north of Grants Pass, 37 miles south of Roseburg, and approximately 220 miles south of Portland on I-5.
