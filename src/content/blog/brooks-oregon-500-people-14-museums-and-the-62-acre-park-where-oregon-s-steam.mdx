## Fourteen Museums in a Town of 500

Brooks doesn't have a city government, a downtown, or even an incorporated boundary. What it has is [Powerland Heritage Park](https://www.antiquepowerland.com/) — a 62-acre complex just off I-5 at Exit 263 that houses **14 hands-on heritage museums** dedicated to the preservation, restoration, and operation of antique equipment, vehicles, steam-powered machinery, trains, trolleys, logging equipment, and farm implements.

The ratio is worth pausing on: roughly one museum for every 36 residents.

Powerland hosts events throughout the year, from *S.T.E.A.M.'d Up for Kids* to *Holiday Sparkles*, but the flagship is **The Great Oregon Steam-Up** — an award-winning annual event where vintage steam engines, tractors, and industrial machinery are fired up and operated for the public. It's the kind of event that draws people who know the difference between a Case and a Rumely, and people who just want to see enormous machines make noise and move.

The Oregon Fire Service Museum, housed at Powerland, showcases the state's firefighting heritage through an architecture inspired by a 1920s two-bay, two-story firehouse. Artifacts range from an 1820 hand pumper to motorized apparatus, with a focus on Oregon-built and Oregon-serving fire trucks.

## The Unincorporated Crossroads

Brooks was a station on the Southern Pacific Railroad main line through the Willamette Valley, named for early settler Linus Brooks, who came to Oregon from Illinois in 1850. The post office was established in 1871, and the community was platted with 30 blocks in 1878 when it had 135 residents. The community never incorporated and never grew appreciably — it remains an unincorporated census-designated place of roughly 500 people.

Brooks sits on French Prairie, the rich agricultural flatland north of Keizer. The surrounding area is comprised of flower farms, berry growers, and produce stands — the working agricultural landscape that has defined this part of the Willamette Valley since the 1840s.

## Getting to Brooks

**I-5 Exit 263** provides direct access to Powerland Heritage Park (quarter mile from the interchange) and the Brooks community.

Brooks is 9 miles north of Salem/Keizer, 8 miles south of Woodburn, and approximately 40 miles south of Portland on I-5.
