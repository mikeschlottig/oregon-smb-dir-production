## The Bingo Hall That Became an Empire

In 1992, the Cow Creek Band of Umpqua Tribe of Indians opened a bingo hall in Canyonville, Oregon. It had 60 machines and 33 employees.

Today, that bingo hall is the [Seven Feathers Casino Resort](https://sevenfeathers.com/about/) — a 298-room, AAA three-star hotel and casino with 232,500 square feet of total space, 68,000 square feet of gaming, over 900 slot machines, table games, a spa, multiple restaurants, an RV resort, and a truck and travel center. It is the largest gaming facility in Southern Oregon. It attracts over one million visitors annually. And standing guard at the main entrance is Skookum Hyak — a larger-than-life bronze eagle that is, at over 33 feet tall and 10,000 pounds, the largest bronze casting of an eagle in the world.

The story of how this happened is the story of the [Cow Creek Band of Umpqua Tribe of Indians](https://www.cowcreek-nsn.gov/tribal-story/) — one of nine federally recognized tribal governments in Oregon, with a history that includes a treaty that paid them less than three cents per acre for 800 square miles of homeland, federal termination in 1954, and restoration in 1982.

The tribe's economic recovery through gaming has been, by any measure, remarkable. The [Oregon Blue Book](https://sos.oregon.gov/blue-book/Pages/national-tribes-cow-creek.aspx) reports that the Cow Creek Tribe employs approximately 1,100 people — making them one of the largest employers in Douglas County. The tribe manages roughly 48,000 acres of ancestral homeland and funds healthcare, education, housing, and public safety services through gaming revenue.

As Oregon's [tribal gaming history started in Canyonville](https://www.nrtoday.com/news/history/oregons-tribal-gaming-history-started-in-canyonville/article_2f40c628-1241-54fe-ba37-05e81b853f16.html), the tribe has always viewed gaming as "a means to an end" — economic development as the path to cultural independence.

## Exit 99: The Perfect Pit Stop

Canyonville's location makes it one of the most strategically useful stops on I-5 in Oregon.

[Exit 99](https://www.oregonlive.com/galleries/MTN35VV53BFBPOO5JIJR2NNPSU/) sits almost exactly halfway between Eugene and Ashland — roughly 110 miles in each direction. For road trippers driving the full Oregon stretch of I-5, it's the natural midpoint break. Seven Feathers has leaned into this positioning with the casino resort, the RV resort, and the 7 Feathers Truck and Travel Center, creating a full-service stop that gives travelers a reason to spend money, not just gas.

The town of Canyonville itself is small — roughly 2,000 people in a narrow valley along the South Umpqua River. It was a stop on the historic canyon route between the Rogue and Umpqua valleys long before the interstate, and the canyon walls that gave the town its name are visible from the highway. Beyond the casino complex, Canyonville has a few local businesses, a small historic downtown, and access to the surrounding Douglas County forest and river country.

## Nearby: South Umpqua Falls and Wine Country

The sacred [South Umpqua Falls](https://sos.oregon.gov/blue-book/Pages/national-tribes-cow-creek.aspx), located near Tiller roughly 30 miles east of Canyonville, holds cultural significance for the Cow Creek Umpqua people. The falls are a dramatic natural feature on the upper South Umpqua River and host an intertribal pow-wow typically held in July, with drumming, dancing, and fresh salmon.

North of Canyonville, the [Umpqua Valley wine region](https://www.umpquavalleywineries.org/wine-facts/) begins in earnest — with over 30 wineries, 70 vineyards, and more than 40 grape varieties planted across one of Oregon's most climatically diverse AVAs. The valley's winegrowing history dates to the 1880s, and Richard Sommer planted Oregon's first Pinot Noir vines near Roseburg in 1961.

## Getting to Canyonville

**I-5 Exit 98** and **Exit 99** both provide access to Canyonville. Exit 99 connects directly to Seven Feathers Casino Resort and the town center.

Canyonville is 23 miles south of Roseburg, 52 miles north of Grants Pass, and approximately 205 miles south of Portland on I-5.
