## The Town at the Crossroads

Here is a town of 19,000 people that is, simultaneously, the home of the best blue cheese on the planet, the gateway to two seven-million-year-old volcanic mesas, and the custodian of a 12,000-square-foot museum where rocks glow in the dark.

Central Point, Oregon, does not appear on most travel itineraries. It sits four miles north of Medford on Interstate 5, and most people who've been through it don't know they have. The exits blend into the Medford metro sprawl. The name doesn't help — it sounds like a geographic descriptor, not a destination.

But the name is, in fact, exactly right. In 1852, a pioneer named Isaac Constant settled at a spot where the main north-south road from the Willamette Valley met the road running west to Jacksonville and east to the Rogue River settlements. He called it Central Point because it was the literal crossroads of the [Rogue Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogue_Valley). The [Oregon Encyclopedia](https://www.oregonencyclopedia.org/articles/central_point/) notes that the crossroads identity has defined the town ever since — first as a trading post, then as an orchard and timber community, and now as the quiet, slightly underestimated center of a valley with far more going on than the interstate suggests.

The question with Central Point isn't whether there's anything here. It's how so many genuinely interesting things ended up in the same small town without anyone outside the Rogue Valley noticing.

## The Cheese That Won the World

The single most famous product to come out of Central Point is a wheel of blue cheese wrapped in grape leaves.

[Rogue Creamery](https://roguecreamery.com/pages/our-story), located on North Front Street, was founded in 1928 as the Rogue River Valley Creamery Cooperative. It operated modestly for decades until 2002, when David Gremmels and Cary Bryant purchased the aging facility on a handshake and began transforming it into one of the most awarded artisan creameries in the world.

Their masterpiece is [Rogue River Blue](https://roguecreamery.com/products/rogue-river-blue-cheese) — a seasonal cheese made exclusively from fall milk, when cooler temperatures and renewed pasture growth produce what Gremmels considers the best milk of the year. Each wheel is aged 9 to 11 months in the creamery's caves, then hand-wrapped in organic, biodynamic Syrah grape leaves that have been soaked in pear spirits. The cheese is released once a year, on the Autumnal Equinox.

In 2019, Rogue River Blue was named [World Champion at the World Cheese Awards](https://oregoncheeseguild.org/rogue-river-blue-wins-worlds-best-cheese/) in Bergamo, Italy — beating more than 3,800 entries from 42 countries. It was the first American cheese ever to win the top prize. The [Oregon Cheese Guild](https://oregoncheeseguild.org/rogue-river-blue-wins-worlds-best-cheese/) called it "the product that led the way and put American cheese on the map."

The tasting room in Central Point is open to visitors. You can sample Rogue River Blue alongside the creamery's other award winners — Smokey Blue, Bluehorn Blue, and the semi-hard Touvelle — and watch production through viewing windows. The annual [Oregon Cheese Festival](https://traveloregon.com/things-to-do/events/culinary-events/get-schooled-at-the-oregon-cheese-festival/), held in Central Point, draws cheese lovers from across the state.

For a town of 19,000, having a World Champion anything is unusual. Having the World Champion cheese is absurd. And yet, there it is, aging quietly in caves on North Front Street.

## Rocks That Glow in the Dark

[Crater Rock Museum](https://craterrock.com/crater-rock-museum/) is one of those places that shouldn't be as good as it is.

Housed at 2002 Scenic Avenue, the museum started in 1954 as a humble shack where members of the Roxy Ann Gem & Mineral Society could compare finds and swap rockhounding stories. It has since grown into a 12,000-square-foot facility that the [Crater Rock Museum](https://craterrock.com/crater-rock-museum/) describes as housing "one of the finest displays of gems, minerals, rocks, and fossils on the West Coast."

The headline attraction is the Fluorescent Mineral Display — a darkened room where you close the curtain, set the timer, and watch an entire wall of rocks erupt in neon color under ultraviolet light. Minerals that look like ordinary gray and brown stones in daylight suddenly glow vivid green, orange, red, and blue. It is, without exaggeration, one of the more surprising things you can experience in a small-town museum anywhere in Oregon.

Beyond the fluorescent room, the museum's permanent exhibits include world-class mineral specimens, an extensive collection of petrified wood, Native American artifacts, fossils spanning hundreds of millions of years, and a gift shop stocked with the work of local lapidary artists. [TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g51800-d2649699-Reviews-Crater_Rock_Museum-Central_Point_Oregon.html) rates it 4.7 out of 5. It's free to visit, though donations are encouraged.

## The Table Rocks and the Rogue River

Central Point's most dramatic natural feature is visible from almost anywhere in town: the flat-topped silhouettes of Upper and Lower Table Rock rising above the valley floor to the northeast.

The [Table Rocks](https://www.blm.gov/programs/recreation/recreation-activities/oregon-washington/tablerocks) — twin volcanic plateaus formed approximately seven million years ago — are managed jointly by the Bureau of Land Management and The Nature Conservancy. The trailheads for both hikes are accessed via Modoc Road and Table Rock Road, a short drive from Central Point's I-5 exits. Upper Table Rock is the more popular hike at 2.5 miles round trip; Lower Table Rock is longer at 6.3 miles and recently received [trail improvements from the BLM](https://www.blm.gov/announcement/blm-reopens-lower-table-rock-trails-after-improvements), including a new accessible Oak Savannah Trail with interpretive panels.

Closer to town, [TouVelle State Recreation Site](https://www.southernoregon.org/cities/central-point/) sits along the Rogue River north of Central Point, offering picnic areas, river access for fishing and swimming, and one of the more scenic stretches of the Rogue in the valley. The [Bear Creek Greenway](https://jacksoncountyor.gov/departments/parks/greenway/bear_creek_greenway.php) passes through Central Point as part of its 20-mile paved route from Ashland to the Table Rocks area, connecting the town to the valley's recreational spine.

Don Jones Memorial Park, the city's largest park, offers sports fields, playgrounds, a skate park, and community gathering space. For a town that functions partly as a bedroom community for Medford, Central Point has invested meaningfully in the kind of public spaces that make residents want to stay.

## The Quiet Economy

Central Point's economy is practical, diversified, and largely invisible from the highway.

The median household income is approximately $80,450 to $86,058, depending on the source — [Data USA](https://datausa.io/profile/geo/central-point-or) and [World Population Review](https://worldpopulationreview.com/us-cities/oregon/central-point) report slightly different figures based on survey year. The poverty rate is 6.73% — significantly lower than Medford's 13.17% — and the median age is 41.7 years, reflecting a community with a mix of families, retirees, and working professionals.

The town functions as part of the greater Medford labor market. Most residents commute to jobs in Medford, Ashland, or elsewhere in Jackson County. Major regional employers — Asante Health System, Lithia Motors, Providence, Harry & David — draw workers from across the valley, and Central Point's relative affordability and family-oriented neighborhoods make it a popular choice for people who work elsewhere but want a quieter home base.

The [Central Point Chamber of Commerce](https://centralpointchamber.org/about-central-point/) positions the town as "the first town in the valley" — a nod to its 1852 origins as the crossroads settlement that predates Medford by three decades. The orchard industry that once defined the area has diminished, but agriculture remains present in the surrounding valley, and the food production heritage lives on through operations like Rogue Creamery and the regional wine industry.

## Housing: The Rogue Valley's Value Play

Central Point's housing market is one of the more straightforward value propositions in the Rogue Valley.

The average home value is approximately $409,768 as of early 2026, according to [Zillow](https://www.zillow.com/home-values/10769/central-point-or/) — up 0.4% year-over-year. The median sale price tracked by [Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/city/3215/OR/Central-Point/housing-market) was around $390,000, though listing prices tend to run higher at roughly $470,000 median ask.

Compared to neighboring Ashland ($550,000+) or even Medford ($397,000), Central Point offers more house for the money — and a median household income that's actually *higher* than Medford's by roughly $7,000–$13,000, depending on the data year. That combination of lower home prices and higher median income means Central Point households face less of the affordability strain that defines the housing conversation in other Rogue Valley cities.

The tradeoff is character. Central Point lacks the cultural density of Ashland, the urban services of Medford, or the dramatic narrative of fire-recovery towns like Talent and Phoenix. What it offers instead is something simpler: reasonable homes, good parks, solid schools, and proximity to everything the valley has — including, on North Front Street, the best cheese in the world.

## Getting to Central Point

**I-5 Exit 33** (Pine Street / Table Rock Road) provides access to Central Point and the road to the Table Rocks trailheads. **Exit 35** (Seven Oaks / OR-99) connects to the southern end of town and the old Pacific Highway corridor.

Central Point is 4 miles north of Medford, 17 miles north of Ashland, and approximately 275 miles south of Portland. [Rogue Valley International-Medford Airport (MFR)](https://www.jacksoncountyor.gov/airport) is 6 miles south.
