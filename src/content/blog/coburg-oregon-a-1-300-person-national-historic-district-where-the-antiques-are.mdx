## The Antique Village That Isn't Stuck in the Past

Coburg is the kind of place that makes you slow down — literally. You exit I-5 at mile 199, and within a minute you're on a two-lane road lined with mature trees, 19th-century storefronts, and a population density that feels more like a Vermont village than a Willamette Valley town.

Founded in 1847 and incorporated in 1893, the entire city of Coburg is a designated National Historic District. The architecture along Willamette Street tells a story of careful preservation — not the aggressive boutique-ification that happens when developers discover a photogenic small town, but genuine continuity. Buildings have been maintained, not reimagined.

The antique malls are the draw. Coburg Antique and Vintage Malls, Coburg Antique Fair, and several smaller dealers have turned this tiny town into a regional destination for collectors, decorators, and weekend browsers from Eugene and beyond. The shops are serious — staffed by knowledgeable dealers who trade in quality pieces, not roadside kitsch.

But here's the number that tells the real story: **the median household income in Coburg is $98,000**, per World Population Review. In a town of 1,365 people. With a poverty rate of 1.48%.

Coburg isn't a struggling small town that found a second life in tourism. It's a quietly affluent community that happens to sit at the northern edge of Lane County, eight miles from the University of Oregon, with excellent schools (Coburg Community Charter School in the 4J district), easy interstate access, and the kind of livability that attracts professionals who want space, character, and a 19-minute average commute to Eugene.

## The Numbers Behind the Charm

The population has been growing at 0.66% annually, per World Population Review — modest but steady for a city that's barely a square mile. The median age is 38.8, reflecting established families rather than retirees. Homeownership rates are high, and the housing stock, while limited, tends toward well-maintained homes on larger lots.

The city government operates out of Coburg City Hall at 91136 N Willamette Street — a building that, like much of Coburg, looks like it belongs in a town that's been doing things right for over a century.

## Getting to Coburg

**I-5 Exit 199** provides direct access to Coburg via Coburg Road. The town center is less than a mile from the interchange.

Coburg is 8 miles north of Eugene, 30 miles south of Albany, and approximately 105 miles south of Portland on I-5.
