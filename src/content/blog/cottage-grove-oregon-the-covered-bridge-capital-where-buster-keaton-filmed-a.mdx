## The Town That Hollywood Keeps Finding

Cottage Grove, Oregon, has appeared in more films than towns ten times its size.

In 1926, Buster Keaton used the local railroad to film *The General* — a silent film now considered one of the greatest movies ever made. In 1973, Ernest Borgnine chased Lee Marvin across the same tracks in *Emperor of the North*. And in 1986, Rob Reiner brought River Phoenix, Wil Wheaton, and Corey Feldman to the [Row River Trail corridor](https://www.blm.gov/visit/row-river-trail) to film *Stand By Me* — the Stephen King adaptation about four boys walking along railroad tracks to find a dead body.

The trestle bridge at Harms Park — one of several locations along the old Oregon Pacific & Eastern Railroad line — is immortalized in all three films. The BLM notes this in its official trail documentation, which is not something most federal land management agencies get to do.

But Cottage Grove's real claim to fame isn't Hollywood. It's bridges.

## The Covered Bridge Capital

Cottage Grove and the surrounding area are home to seven historic covered bridges within approximately 35 miles — the highest concentration in the state and the reason the town markets itself as the "Covered Bridge Capital of Oregon."

The [Cottage Grove Covered Bridge Tour Route](https://www.eugenecascadescoast.org/blog/post/cottage-grove-covered-bridges/) is a self-guided driving loop that takes visitors past bridges spanning the Row River, Mosby Creek, and other local waterways. Most date from the early 20th century and were built to protect the wooden road decks from Oregon's wet winters. Several are listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

The bridges are photogenic, well-maintained, and distributed through rolling farm country and forested foothills that make the drive itself a pleasure. The [Bohemia Gold Mining Museum](https://www.eugenecascadescoast.org/listing/bohemia-gold-mining-museum/2349/) in downtown Cottage Grove provides context for the region's mining history, and Main Street's antique shops and used bookstores offer an afternoon's worth of browsing.

## The Row River Trail

The [Row River Trail](https://www.blm.gov/visit/row-river-trail) is a 15.6-mile paved, multi-use National Recreation Trail that follows the route of the abandoned Oregon Pacific & Eastern Railroad line along the scenic Row River (rhymes with "cow") and Dorena Reservoir. The BLM-managed trail connects downtown Cottage Grove to Dorena Lake, Culp Creek, and the nearby Umpqua National Forest.

The trail is flat, paved, and accessible to cyclists, runners, and walkers of all levels. It passes through farm country, forest, and along the lakeshore, with multiple trailheads and several covered bridges visible from the route. [AllTrails](https://www.alltrails.com/trail/us/oregon/row-river-trail) rates it easy with 633 feet of total elevation gain over the full distance.

[Dorena Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottage_Grove,_Oregon) itself offers boating, fishing, swimming, and camping — a popular summer destination for Lane County families.

## The Numbers

Cottage Grove is the third-largest city in Lane County with a population of approximately 10,700, per [World Population Review](https://worldpopulationreview.com/us-cities/oregon/cottage-grove). The median household income is $80,875, the poverty rate is 10.71%, and the median age is 38.9. The town sits at the southern edge of the Eugene-Springfield metro area's gravitational pull — close enough to commute (20 miles to Eugene), affordable enough to attract young families and retirees.

## Getting to Cottage Grove

**I-5 Exit 174** provides primary access to downtown Cottage Grove and the Row River Trail. **Exit 170** connects to the southern end of town and the route to Dorena Reservoir.

Cottage Grove is 20 miles south of Eugene, 50 miles north of Roseburg, and approximately 130 miles south of Portland on I-5.
