## The Friendly City

Creswell calls itself "The Friendly City," and while that's the kind of slogan every small town claims, in Creswell's case the friendliness is partly a function of scale.

At 5,600 people, Creswell is large enough to have its own schools, parks, a weekly newspaper (*The Creswell Chronicle*), and a general aviation airport (Hobby Field), but small enough that people still say hello on the street. It sits at I-5 Exit 182, ten miles south of Eugene, in the southern Willamette Valley where the flat farmland begins to give way to the rolling foothills of the Coast Range and the Calapooya Mountains.

The town has been growing steadily as part of the Eugene-Springfield metro's expanding footprint. The median property value is approximately $355,100, per [Data USA](https://datausa.io/profile/geo/creswell-or) — significantly more affordable than Eugene proper. The homeownership rate is 73.4%, one of the higher rates in Lane County, reflecting a community that draws families and homeowners who work in Eugene but prefer a smaller-town home base.

Creswell's most famous connection is geographic: the [Oregon Country Fair](https://www.oregoncountryfair.org/), held annually in July in nearby Veneta (13 miles west of Eugene), draws tens of thousands of visitors for a three-day celebration of art, music, food, and counterculture. While the fair is technically in Veneta, Creswell benefits from the regional traffic and its position as the southern gateway to Lane County's event calendar.

Notable Creswell natives include Mark Few, the head coach of the Gonzaga Bulldogs basketball program, and Bertha Holt, who founded Holt International Children's Services.

## Getting to Creswell

**I-5 Exit 182** provides access to Creswell via OR-99. The town center is less than a mile from the interchange.

Creswell is 10 miles south of Eugene, 30 miles north of Cottage Grove, and approximately 120 miles south of Portland on I-5.
