## The Town You Drive Past

Donald is the kind of town that exists mostly as a name on a highway sign. At I-5 Exit 278 — shared with Aurora — the signage reads "Aurora/Donald," and nearly everyone turns toward Aurora and its antique shops. Donald sits to the east, along Butteville Road, in the open agricultural spaces of North Marion County.

A University of Oregon blog once called Donald "the anomaly that is Donald, Oregon" — a tiny incorporated city nestled within open spaces, just off Exit 278, that you would cruise past without a second glance. The characterization is accurate. Donald is 0.379 square miles, with a population of approximately 1,013 that has barely changed since the 2020 census.

But Donald is not abandoned or declining. The **homeownership rate is 88.1%**, per Data USA, with a median property value of $336,500. The median household income is $79,167, with a poverty rate of 7.06%. The average commute time is 28.1 minutes — suggesting residents work in Woodburn, Salem, or the southern Portland metro.

## Harvest Gardens and the Future

Donald's most notable recent development is the **Harvest Gardens Agrihood** — a 110,000-square-foot speculative industrial building with office space and loading docks, completed in 2024 at 21270 Butteville Road. The project represents a new kind of development for a tiny agricultural town: industrial capacity designed to serve the region's agricultural and food processing economy.

The I-5 Exit 278 interchange itself is undergoing a major ODOT reconstruction into a diverging diamond interchange — a four-year project that will improve safety and traffic flow for both Donald and Aurora. The investment signals that this interchange, shared by two tiny towns, is becoming increasingly important to regional transportation.

## Getting to Donald

**I-5 Exit 278** (Aurora/Donald) provides access. Donald is located east of the interchange along Butteville Road.

Donald is adjacent to Aurora, 5 miles north of Woodburn, and approximately 25 miles south of Portland on I-5.
