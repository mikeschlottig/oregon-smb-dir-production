## The Town Named for a Person, Not a Plumbing Feature

Let's address this immediately: Drain, Oregon, is named for [Charles Drain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drain,_Oregon), a local pioneer and benefactor who donated land for the town. It has nothing to do with water drainage, though the town does sit at the convergence of Elk Creek and Pass Creek, both tributaries of the Umpqua River. The naming confusion has likely cost the town at least a few curious visitors over the years.

Drain is a city of 1,200 people at I-5 Exit 162, sitting at a crossroads that has defined the community since its founding. [Oregon Route 38](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Route_38) departs from Drain and heads west through the Coast Range to Reedsport on the Pacific Ocean — making this tiny town the official gateway from the I-5 corridor to the southern Oregon coast. OR-99 also passes through, connecting to the historic Pacific Highway corridor.

The [City of Drain](https://drainoregon.org/) proudly notes an unusual historical distinction: the last California Condor sighting in Oregon occurred in Drain in 1904. The condors never returned. The town, however, has maintained a remarkably stable population of 1,000 to 1,200 for over 60 years.

## Victorian Charm and Covered Bridges

Drain has a collection of Victorian-era houses and historic buildings that reflect its 19th-century origins. The town was founded around a Normal School (teacher training college) and the railroad, though it eventually lost the school to Monmouth and the direct rail line to Eugene.

[Pass Creek Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drain,_Oregon), a covered bridge located in a park behind the Drain Civic Center, was formerly a road bridge before the city moved it to the park in 1987 and opened it for pedestrian traffic. The bridge was closed in 2014 due to deteriorating support timbers, but it remains a visual landmark and a connection to Douglas County's covered bridge heritage.

The surrounding hills offer the kind of quiet, affordable rural life that the [city's website](https://drainoregon.org/) promotes directly: "Victorian houses, covered bridges, scenic highways and community activities provide many attractions for tourists. Affordable housing, low tax rates in Douglas County and a low cost of living make Drain one of the most desirable retirement communities in Oregon."

The median household income is $46,103, with a poverty rate of 35.26%, per [World Population Review](https://worldpopulationreview.com/us-cities/oregon/drain) — one of the highest poverty rates of any city on the I-5 corridor. Drain is honest about what it is: a small, affordable, and very quiet place where the University of Oregon is less than an hour away, the coast is an hour west, and the cost of everything is a fraction of what it is in the valley's larger cities.

## Getting to Drain

**I-5 Exit 162** provides access to Drain and OR-38 west to the coast. The drive to Reedsport on the Pacific is approximately 55 miles.

Drain is 35 miles north of Roseburg, 35 miles south of Eugene, and approximately 145 miles south of Portland on I-5.
