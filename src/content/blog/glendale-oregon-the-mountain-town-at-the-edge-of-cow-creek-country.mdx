## Where the Counties Change

Glendale is where you cross from Josephine County into Douglas County on Interstate 5 — and where the landscape announces, with no subtlety, that the Rogue Valley is behind you.

The town sits at roughly 1,400 feet in a narrow mountain valley along Cow Creek, surrounded by steep forested slopes of the Siskiyou and Cascade foothills. At 900 people, it is one of the smallest incorporated cities on the Oregon I-5 corridor. The exit — number 80 — leads to a main street that can be driven end to end in about a minute. There is a general store, a few churches, a school, and the kind of quiet that settles into small mountain towns when the timber industry leaves.

But Glendale's smallness belies its significance. This is the heart of [Cow Creek country](https://www.cowcreek-nsn.gov/tribal-story/) — the ancestral territory of the Nahankhuotana people, now federally recognized as the [Cow Creek Band of Umpqua Tribe of Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cow_Creek_Band_of_Umpqua_Tribe_of_Indians).

## The Cow Creek Story

The Cow Creek Umpqua people's ancestral land stretches from the Umpqua and Rogue River drainages to the Cascade and Coastal Mountain Ranges. The [Oregon Blue Book](https://sos.oregon.gov/blue-book/Pages/national-tribes-cow-creek.aspx) describes how, in 1853, the Nahankhuotana Tribe relinquished rights to their homeland in one of the first treaties signed with the United States — receiving $12,000 for nearly 800 square miles of land, at less than three cents per acre.

The treaty's aftermath was devastating. Many tribal members were sent to reservations. Others fled into the mountains, where they hid and avoided displaying their cultural traditions for decades. In 1954, the federal government terminated its recognition of the tribe entirely.

But the Cow Creek people persisted. Tribal families organized, maintained their identity, and in December 1982, the U.S. Government re-recognized the Cow Creek Band of Umpqua Tribe of Indians. Today, the tribe is one of nine federally recognized Tribal Governments in Oregon, with over 2,000 members and a Tribal government headquartered in Roseburg. The [Oregon Travel Information Council](https://oregontic.com/oregon-historical-markers/cow-creeks-a-tale-of-strong-recovery/) maintains a historical marker telling the story.

The tribe's [Seven Feathers Casino Resort](https://www.sevenfeathers.com/) in Canyonville — 18 miles north of Glendale on I-5 — is one of the most visible expressions of the Cow Creek Umpqua's economic recovery. But the story begins here, in the creek valleys and mountain forests around Glendale.

## The Back Country Byway

The best way to experience Glendale's landscape is to leave I-5.

The [Cow Creek Back Country Byway](https://www.blm.gov/sites/default/files/documents/files/rbg_cowcreek_brochure.pdf), managed by the Bureau of Land Management, is a 45-mile scenic drive on a rustic two-lane road connecting Glendale to the town of Riddle to the north. The byway follows Cow Creek through steep canyon country, passing signs about the forgotten town of West Fork, historic gold mining claims, and the active railroad that still follows the original Oregon and California Railroad grade built in 1869.

Along the way, the **Island Creek day-use area** offers a stop for stretching, picnicking, or panning for gold in the creek — a legal and popular activity along the byway. **Skull Creek Campground** provides five free, first-come-first-served sites for travelers willing to trade amenities for solitude.

The byway is not fast. Logging trucks use the road, blind corners are common, and portions of the surrounding forest were burned in the Douglas Complex Fire of 2013. But for anyone interested in Oregon's mining history, railroad heritage, or simply the experience of driving through canyon country that hasn't changed much in a century, it's one of the most rewarding detours between Grants Pass and Roseburg.

## Getting to Glendale

**I-5 Exit 80** provides access to Glendale. The town center is less than a mile from the interchange.

Glendale is 22 miles north of Grants Pass, 45 miles north of Medford, and approximately 228 miles south of Portland on I-5. It sits at the southern end of the Cow Creek Back Country Byway.
