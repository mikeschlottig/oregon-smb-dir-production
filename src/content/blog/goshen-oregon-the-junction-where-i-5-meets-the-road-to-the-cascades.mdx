## The Fork in the Road

Goshen is where the road splits.

At I-5 Exit 186, the Willamette Highway (OR-58) departs eastward from the interstate and begins its climb into the Cascade Range. The highway passes through Oakridge — a mountain biking and outdoor recreation hub — crosses Willamette Pass at 5,128 feet, and descends to Klamath Falls and Central Oregon. For travelers heading to Crater Lake from the north, or connecting from the Willamette Valley to Highway 97, OR-58 is one of the primary cross-Cascade routes in Oregon.

Goshen itself is an unincorporated community of roughly 2,300 people in the flat agricultural country south of Eugene. It has no downtown, no city government, and no commercial center beyond the services clustered near the interchange. What it has is position — the exact point where the Willamette Valley's north-south corridor meets the east-west route into the mountains.

The surrounding area is rural Lane County farmland, with the Coast Fork of the Willamette River running nearby. Residents are within easy commuting distance of both Eugene and Springfield, making Goshen a quiet, affordable alternative to the metro's denser neighborhoods.

## Getting to Goshen

**I-5 Exit 186** provides access to Goshen and the junction with OR-58 east to Oakridge, Willamette Pass, and Klamath Falls.

Goshen is 8 miles south of Eugene, 6 miles north of Creswell, and approximately 115 miles south of Portland on I-5.
