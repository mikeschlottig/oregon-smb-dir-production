## Named for a Straight Line

Halsey exists because of geometry.

In 1870 and 1871, the Oregon & California Railroad pushed its line south through the Willamette Valley. Vice-President William L. Halsey oversaw construction, and when the company established a station along a remarkable stretch of track — more than 20 miles without a single curve — the town took his name. The federal government established a post office in Halsey on September 17, 1872, and the city incorporated in 1876.

The name "Tangent" — the next town north on the same rail line — refers to the same geometric feature. Two towns, both named for the straightest stretch of railroad in the valley. The Willamette Valley's flat topography made it possible; the railroad engineers named the evidence.

## Fire, Grain, and Gunpowder

Halsey's history reads like a series of dramatic footnotes.

On July 31, 1903, a fire started at the J. L. Bishur Creamery and burned most of the city's downtown core to the ground. The town rebuilt — smaller, more modest, but persistent.

The city's iconic 180-foot grain elevator, built in 1955, stood as the tallest structure in Halsey for decades before being partially demolished in December 2012 due to structural concerns. For a town of under 1,000 people, a 180-foot grain elevator was a statement — the agricultural economy made visible on the skyline.

And then there's this: in 2022, Halsey residents James Tow and Keith Tow developed and introduced the .500 Bushwhacker, which they claim is the most powerful handgun cartridge in the world. Small towns contain multitudes.

## The Surprising Numbers

Halsey's population is approximately 950, per World Population Review, and declining slightly (-0.21% annually). But the median household income is **$106,458** — one of the highest of any town on the I-5 corridor between Eugene and Salem.

The poverty rate is 7.75%, the median age is 35.2, and the community is 77% White with a growing Hispanic population. Halsey is half a square mile of Linn County farmland that punches well above its weight economically, likely reflecting the surrounding agricultural industry's prosperity and the commute access to both Albany and Corvallis.

## Getting to Halsey

**I-5 Exit 216** provides access to Halsey via OR-228 (Halsey-Sweet Home Highway). The town center is less than a mile from the interchange.

Halsey is 15 miles south of Albany, 30 miles north of Eugene, and approximately 85 miles south of Portland on I-5.
