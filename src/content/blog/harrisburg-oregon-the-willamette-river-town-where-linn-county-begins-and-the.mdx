## Where Linn County Begins

Harrisburg is the first Linn County town you reach heading north on I-5 after leaving the Eugene-Springfield metro. The county line is the dividing line — south is Lane County, the university orbit, the Eugene vibe. North is Linn County: agricultural, pragmatic, and proud of the distinction.

At I-5 Exit 209, Harrisburg sits on the east bank of the Willamette River in the flat, fertile heart of the mid-Willamette Valley. The town has been here since the 1850s, growing slowly and deliberately from a settlement of 1,311 in 1970 to approximately 3,750 today. That's a 44% jump between 1990 and 2000, followed by steadier growth that reflects new housing development and families drawn to affordable property in the expanding Eugene commute shed.

The median household income is $73,650, per World Population Review, with a poverty rate of 8.86% — solid, middle-class numbers for a town this size. The median age is 33.8, notably young, suggesting Harrisburg is attracting working families rather than retirees.

Harrisburg's riverfront park along the Willamette is a point of civic pride, offering green space and river access in a town that has always defined itself by its relationship to the water. The Willamette here is wide, slow, and navigable — the same river that drew settlers in the 1840s and 1850s, when water transport was the primary means of moving goods through the valley.

## A Growing Town in a Quiet Stretch

The stretch of I-5 between Eugene and Albany is one of the quietest on the entire Oregon corridor. The landscape is flat Willamette Valley farmland — grass seed fields, hay, livestock, and the occasional industrial operation. Harrisburg is the largest community in this stretch, serving as a modest commercial center for the surrounding agricultural area.

The town has its own schools, a small commercial district along Highway 99E, and the infrastructure of a community that expects to keep growing. The City of Harrisburg tracks its population growth carefully, publishing historical data going back to 1970 — the kind of attention to trajectory that suggests a town planning for its next chapter, not just maintaining its current one.

## Getting to Harrisburg

**I-5 Exit 209** provides access to Harrisburg. The town center is approximately one mile east of the interchange.

Harrisburg is 20 miles north of Eugene, 25 miles south of Albany, and approximately 95 miles south of Portland on I-5.
