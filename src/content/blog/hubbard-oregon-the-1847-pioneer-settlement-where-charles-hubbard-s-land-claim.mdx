## The Pioneer Who Chose Oregon Over Texas

In 1847, Charles Hubbard Jr. and his family faced a decision at Independence, Missouri: head to the new state of Texas, or the territory of Oregon. They chose Oregon.

The journey nearly killed them. Arriving at the Whitman Mission in October, the family had to choose between wintering over or pressing on through cold and frost to Oregon City. They pressed on — arriving in December 1847. It was a fortunate choice. Twelve people who stayed at the Whitman Mission, along with its founders Narcissa and Marcus Whitman, were killed by Cayuse warriors in November.

The following spring, Charles Hubbard visited Thomas Hunt's squatter's claim on the ridge between the Pudding River and Mill Creek. Hunt wanted to seek his fortune in the California Gold Rush, so he encouraged Hubbard to rent and maintain the claim. Hunt left, never to be heard from again. Hubbard eventually acquired 400 acres and offered 10 acres as an inducement for the Oregon & California Railroad, which was under construction from 1868-1878. The town that grew around the station took his name.

## Less Than a Square Mile, More Than You'd Expect

Hubbard today is 0.8 square miles of Marion County with a population of approximately 3,386, per World Population Review. The numbers are quietly impressive: the **median household income is $101,771** with a **poverty rate of just 3.55%**. Nearly half (49.5%) of all households have children under 18, and 59.1% are married-couple households.

The median age is 34.1 — young, family-oriented, and working. Hubbard's density is 4,249 people per square mile, reflecting a compact, walkable town center along Highway 99E.

The city sits between Woodburn (5 miles south) and Aurora (2 miles north), in the agricultural heartland of northern Marion County. The I-5 Exit 278 interchange at Aurora/Donald is undergoing a major reconstruction into a diverging diamond interchange — a multi-year ODOT project designed to address the traffic congestion that Hubbard's growing region has created.

## Getting to Hubbard

**I-5 Exit 278** (Aurora/Donald) provides the nearest interstate access. Hubbard is located along Highway 99E, approximately 2 miles east of the interchange.

Hubbard is 5 miles north of Woodburn, 2 miles south of Aurora, and approximately 25 miles south of Portland on I-5.
