## The Quiet Exit

Hugo is the kind of place that makes you wonder who named it and why.

The answer, as with many Oregon place names, involves a railroad. [Hugo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo,_Oregon) is an unincorporated community in Josephine County, situated along the old Oregon and California Railroad corridor north of Grants Pass. The settlement grew around a railroad stop in the late 19th century and was named — according to local lore — for Hugo Carstanjen, a community member in the area's early days.

Today, Hugo is I-5 Exit 66: a rural interchange between Grants Pass (8 miles south) and Wolf Creek (10 miles north). There is no commercial district. No gas station. No restaurant. The exit serves a scattered residential community in the forested hills of northern Josephine County, and it functions primarily as a connector for locals and a brief interruption in the rhythm of interstate driving.

What Hugo offers — and this is not nothing — is the landscape transition itself. South of here, the Rogue Valley is open, agricultural, sun-warmed. North of here, the terrain compresses. The Siskiyou Mountains close in. The interstate begins its winding climb through the canyon country that separates Southern Oregon from the Umpqua Valley. Hugo sits at the seam.

## What's Here

Hugo has a post office (ZIP 97532, shared with Merlin), a volunteer fire department, and the kind of rural character that resists description because it resists development. The surrounding land is a mix of private timber, small acreages, and BLM-managed forest. The community's proximity to Grants Pass means that residents have access to city services without living in the city — a tradeoff that defines many of Oregon's unincorporated rural communities.

The [ODOT I-5 improvement project](https://www.oregon.gov/odot/projects/pages/project-details.aspx?project=21674) between Hugo and Grants Pass, which began in 2025, includes pavement improvements and hazard tree removal at Exit 66 — a reminder that even the quietest exits require maintenance.

For I-5 travelers, Hugo is the kind of exit you notice only because the sign says something. But for the people who live here, that quiet is precisely the point.

## Getting to Hugo

**I-5 Exit 66** (Monument Drive) provides access to Hugo. The exit connects to local roads serving the surrounding rural community.

Hugo is 8 miles north of Grants Pass, 38 miles northwest of Medford, and approximately 242 miles south of Portland on I-5.
