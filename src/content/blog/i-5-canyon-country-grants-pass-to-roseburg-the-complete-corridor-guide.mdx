## The Canyon Country Corridor

This is the wildest stretch of I-5 in Oregon. Between Grants Pass and Roseburg, the interstate carves through narrow canyons, climbs over ridges, and passes through some of the smallest, most remote communities on the entire corridor. The towns here are measured in hundreds, not thousands — places where the freeway is the lifeline and the surrounding mountains are both the beauty and the barrier.

Ten communities span this 63-mile corridor, from the rural outposts of Merlin and Hugo to the Douglas County seat of Roseburg.

## Corridor Themes

- **Canyon Terrain:** This is the most geographically dramatic stretch of I-5 in Oregon — steep grades, narrow passes, and communities wedged into canyon bottoms.

- **Timber Legacy:** Nearly every town in this corridor was built on timber, and nearly every town is still reckoning with the industry's decline.

- **Tribal Economy:** The Cow Creek Band of Umpqua Tribe's Seven Feathers Casino transformed Canyonville's economy.

- **The Umpqua Valley:** Roseburg anchors a wine and agriculture region that competes with the Rogue Valley for quality and is catching up on recognition.
