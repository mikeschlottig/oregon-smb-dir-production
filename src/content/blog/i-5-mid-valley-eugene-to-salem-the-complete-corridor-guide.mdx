## The Mid-Valley Corridor

This is the quietest stretch of I-5 in Oregon — and the most economically productive.

The mid-Willamette Valley between Eugene and Salem is the global center of grass seed production, one of Oregon's richest agricultural regions, and home to communities that range from 950-person railroad towns to the 182,600-person state capital. The landscape is flat, the towns are spaced evenly along the old railroad line, and the median incomes are surprisingly high.

Ten communities span this 61-mile corridor.

## Corridor Themes

- **Agricultural Powerhouse:** The Willamette Valley between Eugene and Salem is the global center of grass seed production and Oregon's top agricultural region.

- **Railroad Heritage:** Halsey and Tangent are both named for the same 20-mile straight stretch of railroad track — geometry made into town names.

- **Historic Preservation:** Albany and Coburg demonstrate what happens when towns preserve rather than demolish their 19th-century architecture.

- **Government & Capital:** Salem anchors the corridor as Oregon's political center, with Willamette University adding academic prestige.

- **Hidden Wealth:** Millersburg ($124K), Halsey ($106K), Jefferson ($102K), and Turner ($102K) carry median incomes that rival Portland suburbs.
