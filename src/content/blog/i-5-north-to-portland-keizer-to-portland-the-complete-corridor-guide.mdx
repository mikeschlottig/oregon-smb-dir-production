## The North to Portland Corridor

This is where Oregon transforms from agricultural valley to metropolitan region. Heading north from Keizer, the landscape shifts from flat farmland to increasingly dense suburban development, the town populations grow from hundreds to hundreds of thousands, and the economic character pivots from agriculture and government to technology, manufacturing, and the creative economy.

Eleven communities span this 45-mile corridor — the shortest distance, the most population, and the greatest economic diversity of any segment.

## Corridor Themes

- **Agricultural to Metropolitan:** The most dramatic economic transition on the corridor — from Brooks' 500 residents and heritage museums to Portland's 627,000 people and global food scene.

- **Cultural Diversity:** Woodburn (60%+ Latino) and Aurora (utopian colony heritage) represent Oregon's immigrant and idealist traditions.

- **Silicon Forest:** Tualatin and Tigard anchor the southern edge of Portland's semiconductor and tech corridor.

- **Wealth Gradient:** Lake Oswego ($141K median) is the wealthiest I-5 community; Portland ($91K) and Woodburn ($67K) show the metro's economic range.

- **Transit Innovation:** Wilsonville's fare-free SMART system and Portland's TriMet/MAX represent different approaches to public transit.
