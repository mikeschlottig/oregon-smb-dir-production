## The Ferry Crossing That Became a Town

Jefferson began as a river crossing.

In 1851, Jacob Conser moved to the north bank of the Santiam River and built a ferry. The location was strategic — one mile below the junction of the north and south forks of the Santiam, nine miles above the confluence with the Willamette. The only road through the area was a wagon trail to Santiam City, a community that would later be washed away during the catastrophic flood of 1861. Conser's crossing survived.

Conser didn't just build a ferry. He built a sawmill, an education center, and a flour and grist mill. In 1854, he constructed the Conser House — the first framed building in Jefferson — which served as both his home and a hotel. General Phil Sheridan and Ben Holladay, the noted railroad builder, stayed there. Conser went on to become a state legislator and later a Marion County judge.

The town that grew around Conser's Ferry was first known as Conser's Ferry before taking the name Jefferson. It sits on the east bank of the Santiam River, between Salem and Albany along Oregon Route 164, east of Interstate 5.

## The Santiam Gateway

Jefferson's identity is inseparable from the Santiam River. The Santiam drains a large swath of the western Cascade Range, carrying snowmelt and rain from the mountains through the foothills and into the Willamette Valley. By the time it reaches Jefferson, the river is a broad, accessible waterway — good for fishing, floating, and the kind of quiet riverside recreation that doesn't require a guidebook.

The river is also a dividing line. South of the Santiam is Linn County — Albany's territory. North of the Santiam is Marion County — Salem's orbit. Jefferson sits precisely at this boundary, part of the Salem Metropolitan Statistical Area but geographically and economically connected to Albany as well.

## Young, Affordable, Surprisingly Prosperous

Jefferson's population is approximately 3,279, per World Population Review, declining slightly at -0.24% annually. But the financial picture is strong: the **median household income is $102,159**, with a poverty rate of 9.21%.

The median age is remarkably young at 31.2 years — one of the youngest of any town on the I-5 corridor — suggesting a community of young families drawn by affordable housing within commuting distance of both Salem and Albany. The male median age is just 28.2, while the female median age is 33, indicating a working-age population rather than a retirement community.

Jefferson is less than a square mile in area (0.857 sq mi) with a density of 3,884 people per square mile — compact, walkable, and contained within clear boundaries.

## Getting to Jefferson

**I-5 Exit 238** (South Jefferson/OR-164) provides access to Jefferson. The town is approximately 2 miles east of the interchange, along OR-164.

Jefferson is 15 miles south of Salem, 10 miles north of Albany, and approximately 60 miles south of Portland on I-5.
