## Portland's Golden Suburb

Lake Oswego is the most affluent city on the I-5 corridor in Oregon, and it's not particularly close.

The **median household income is $141,549**, per World Population Review. The **mean household income is $195,728**, per Forbes. The **median home value is $894,000**, per Data USA. The **poverty rate is 3.44%**. Seventy-five percent of adult residents hold a bachelor's degree or higher — 34% hold a master's degree or above.

Forbes ranked Lake Oswego among the wealthiest places in Oregon, noting it as the largest city in the state's top 10 with 16,649 households. The Lake Oswego Review reported that the city's population grew modestly for the third consecutive year in 2025, reaching 41,474 — a community that is neither booming nor declining, but carefully maintaining its character.

The city's defining feature is Oswego Lake, a private lake accessible only to members of the Lake Oswego Corporation. The lake and its surrounding neighborhoods are the physical center of the city's identity — a 405-acre body of water that shapes property values, social geography, and the community's reputation. Access to the lake is not public, a fact that has generated decades of debate about exclusivity, public access, and what it means to build a community around a private amenity.

## Education and Demographics

Lake Oswego's schools are consistently ranked among the best in Oregon, and the school system is a primary driver of the city's housing market. Families move to Lake Oswego for the schools, and the schools maintain their quality because the families who move there prioritize education.

The demographics reflect this: the median age is 46.7, older than most metro suburbs, with 24% of residents over 65. The population is 76% White, 9% Asian, 7% two or more races, and 6% Hispanic. The city is less diverse than the Portland metro average but more diverse than its reputation suggests, with a growing Asian and multiracial population.

## The Iron Industry Origins

Lake Oswego's history is more industrial than its current wealth implies. The city began as a company town for the Oregon Iron Company, which built a smelter on the shores of Sucker Lake (later renamed Oswego Lake) in the 1860s. The iron industry declined, the lake became a recreation amenity, and the community gradually transformed into the residential suburb it is today.

George Rogers Park, on the Willamette River at the mouth of Oswego Creek, preserves the remains of the old iron furnace — a reminder that this community of $894,000 median homes was once a place where men smelted iron in the Oregon rain.

## Getting to Lake Oswego

**I-5 Exit 290** (Lower Boones Ferry Road) provides the most direct I-5 access. OR-43 (Macadam Avenue/State Street) connects Lake Oswego to downtown Portland along the Willamette River.

Lake Oswego is 8 miles south of downtown Portland, directly adjacent to Tigard, West Linn, and the Dunthorpe neighborhood of Portland.
