## The Launch Point

Merlin is not a town you visit. It's a town you leave from.

This unincorporated community at I-5 Exit 61 — three miles north of Grants Pass — exists, for most visitors, as the place where the pavement narrows and the Rogue River starts to get serious. Take the exit, turn west on Merlin Road, and within minutes you're passing the headquarters of half a dozen rafting outfitters. Keep driving along the Galice Road and the valley tightens, the forest closes in, and the river beside you transforms from the gentle, swimmable Rogue of Grants Pass into something with more current, more rocks, and more consequences.

The [Bureau of Land Management](https://www.blm.gov/programs/national-conservation-lands/oregon-washington/rogue-wsr) divides the Wild and Scenic Rogue into two sections. The **Hellgate Recreation Area** — 27 miles from the mouth of the Applegate River to Grave Creek — is the accessible stretch, popular for day trips with 11 boat ramps and Class I-II rapids. No permits required for private boaters. The **Wild Rogue** — 32.4 miles from Grave Creek to Watson Creek — is the legendary section: Class III with a few Class IVs, typically traveled in 3 to 4 days by raft or 5 days on foot via the Rogue River Trail. This section requires permits, allocated through a [lottery system on ](https://www.recreation.gov/permits/251982)[Recreation.gov](http://Recreation.gov) that fills months in advance.

Merlin and the Galice Road corridor are where both sections begin. Outfitters like [Orange Torpedo Trips](http://www.orangetorpedo.com/location-merlin-oregon/) (headquartered at 210 Merlin Road), [Morrison's Rogue Wilderness Adventures](https://rogueriverraft.com/), and [ARTA River Trips](https://www.arta.org/river-trips/rogue/) stage their operations here — storing boats, running shuttles, briefing clients, and launching trips that range from half-day whitewater runs to multi-day wilderness expeditions with riverside camping under old-growth forest.

## The Road to Galice

The drive itself is worth the trip.

Galice Road follows the Rogue River west from Merlin through increasingly rugged canyon country. The road passes through the small community of Galice — once a gold mining settlement, now little more than a boat ramp and a few residences — before reaching the Grave Creek put-in, where the Wild Rogue officially begins.

Along the way, the river is accessible at multiple points for fishing, swimming, and picnicking. The stretch between Merlin and Galice is one of the most scenic drives in Josephine County — and one of the least known to travelers who stick to I-5.

For anglers, the Rogue near Merlin offers productive water for steelhead and salmon, with guided drift boat trips available from local outfitters year-round.

## Not Quite a Town

Merlin has no city government, no downtown, and no municipal services. It's an unincorporated community — a loose collection of rural residences, small farms, outfitter businesses, and a few commercial properties along Merlin Road.

What it has is location. The community sits at the exact point where the Rogue Valley's comfortable, accessible river culture transitions into genuine wilderness. For the thousands of people who raft the Wild Rogue each year, Merlin is the first and last taste of civilization — the place where you buy ice, check your gear, and say goodbye to cell service for three days.

## Getting to Merlin

**I-5 Exit 61** provides access to Merlin. Turn west (left from northbound, right from southbound) onto Merlin Road and follow it 3.2 miles to the community center and outfitter corridor. Continue west on Galice Road for river access and the Grave Creek put-in.

Merlin is 3 miles north of Grants Pass, 33 miles northwest of Medford, and approximately 247 miles south of Portland.
