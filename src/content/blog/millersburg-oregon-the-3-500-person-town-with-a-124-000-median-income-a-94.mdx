## The Town That Said Yes

In March 2026, the Statesman Journal published a feature asking how a small Oregon town became a hub for industrial development on I-5. The answer was Millersburg.

Linn County Commissioner Roger Nyquist told the paper that while some governments get "caught up in red tape and rule books" that make development difficult, Millersburg city leaders welcome businesses. "How can we get to yes for you on this?" Nyquist said.

When Millersburg uses the slogan "We Are Open for Business" on its website, it's not just a line. Few cities along Oregon's predominant north-south arterial highway have experienced the city's level of industrial growth, and certainly none of its size.

The formula is straightforward: large tracts of developable land, low taxes, easy access to Interstate 5 and rail service, proximity to Albany Municipal Airport, and a city government that treats business development as municipal policy rather than municipal burden. Industrial development, the city argues, means increased well-paying jobs for Millersburg residents, Linn County, and the greater mid-Willamette Valley — plus increased property tax revenue that funds enhanced city services.

## The Numbers Are Remarkable

Millersburg's statistics read like a developer's fantasy.

The population is approximately 3,541, per World Population Review, growing at **2.76% annually** — one of the fastest rates of any city on the I-5 corridor. The population has increased 14.08% since the 2020 census.

The **median household income is $124,101**, per Data USA. The **median property value is $498,700** (up 9.17% in one year). The **homeownership rate is 94.2%** — meaning nearly every household in Millersburg owns their home. The poverty rate is 7.2%.

These numbers describe a community of well-paid homeowners living on relatively large lots in a semi-rural setting, with industrial employment nearby and easy commute access to Albany, Corvallis, and Salem. Millersburg isn't a bedroom community — it's a place where people both live and work, often in the same zip code.

## Growth by Design

Millersburg's industrial growth hasn't happened by accident. The city has actively positioned itself as the Willamette Valley's preferred destination for manufacturers, logistics companies, and industrial operations that need I-5 access without big-city costs and regulations.

The city sits on the east side of I-5 at Exit 234, adjacent to Albany's northern boundary. The land is flat, available, and zoned for industrial use. The Albany Municipal Airport is nearby. Rail service is accessible. And the city government, by all accounts, moves fast.

The result is a town that's simultaneously one of the smallest and one of the wealthiest on the corridor — a place where 3,500 people live in a landscape of new homes, industrial parks, and the kind of quiet prosperity that doesn't make headlines until someone asks where all the growth went.

## Getting to Millersburg

**I-5 Exit 234** provides access to Millersburg via OR-99E. The city stretches along the east side of I-5, north of Albany.

Millersburg is directly adjacent to Albany, 23 miles south of Salem, and approximately 68 miles south of Portland on I-5.
