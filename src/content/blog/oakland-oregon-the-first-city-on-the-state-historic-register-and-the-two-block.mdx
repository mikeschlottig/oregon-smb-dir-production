## Two Blocks, 140 Years

Oakland, Oregon, has a two-block downtown made entirely of original 19th-century brick buildings. It is the best-preserved collection of classic brick commercial architecture in the state — and almost nobody outside Douglas County knows it exists.

The [City of Oakland](https://www.oaklandoregon.org/) was the first city in Oregon placed on the state's historic register, in May 1968. The [Oakland Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland_Historic_District_(Oakland,_Oregon)) was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1979. Over 80 properties in the city were constructed between 1852 and 1890.

The story of how they survived is partly about luck. Oakland was originally established in 1852 on a bluff above Calapooya Creek. In 1871-72, when the Oregon and California Railroad built its line through the valley, the entire town was physically moved — houses dragged on skids by horse teams — to meet the railroad a mile downstream. Fires in 1893 and 1899 destroyed the original wooden commercial buildings. They were replaced by the brick structures that stand today.

Then the town stopped growing. The railroad eventually lost its dominance. I-5 bypassed the town. Without growth pressure, there was no incentive to demolish the old buildings. The brick downtown simply persisted — a two-block time capsule that filmmakers discovered decades later. *Fire in the Sky*, *Grand Tour: Disaster in Time*, and other productions have used Oakland's streets as period-accurate sets.

[Stearns Hardware](https://www.oaklandoregon.org/) has operated continuously since 1887 in its original 1891 building — making it one of the longest-running businesses in the state. The Oakland Museum tells the town's story. Antique shops and the Lamplighter Cafe fill the remaining storefronts.

## Getting to Oakland

**I-5 Exit 138** (northbound) or **Exit 140** (southbound) provides access to Oakland — less than 2 miles from the interstate.

Oakland is 15 miles north of Roseburg, 55 miles south of Eugene, and approximately 165 miles south of Portland on I-5.
