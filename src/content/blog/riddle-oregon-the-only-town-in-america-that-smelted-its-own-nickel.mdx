## The Mountain Made of Nickel

Four miles west of Riddle, Oregon, there is a mountain that contains nickel.

Not trace amounts. Not an interesting geological footnote. [Nickel Mountain](https://oregondiscovery.com/nickel-mountain) held a deposit approximately 3,000 by 6,000 feet, averaging 60 feet in depth, with an average nickel grade of 1.2%. The nickel mineral garnierite — a complex magnesium silicate — was discovered here by sheepherders in 1864. A mine opened in 1882. But it wasn't until 1954 that the [Hanna Mining Company](https://www.oregon.gov/dogami/milo/archive/HistoricalSociety/Josephine/TheHannaNickelOperation.pdf) began commercial production of ferronickel — the first produced in the United States from domestic ores.

For over three decades, the Riddle nickel smelter was the only facility of its kind in the country. The [USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/bul/0931i/report.pdf) documented the deposit as containing over 600,000 tons of ore with a nickel content of 1% or more. A short spur railroad — just 1.5 miles long — connected the mine to the Southern Pacific's Siskiyou Line, and [that short line hauled 100% of the nickel smelted in the United States](https://www.trainorders.com/discussion/read.php?11,3155051) during the mine's operating years.

Production ended in 1987. The mine was repurposed for slag extraction and, eventually, opened to rockhounders. Today, [Nickel Mountain](https://oregondiscovery.com/nickel-mountain) is a known collecting site where visitors can hunt for garnierite (green nickel silicate) and the rarer chrysoprase among piles of mine tailings. You'll need a hammer, a chisel, and a willingness to crack open rusty limonite to find the green traces inside.

## Cow Creek Valley

Riddle sits in the Cow Creek Valley — a narrow, forested corridor that runs through Douglas County's hill country between the I-5 corridor and the backcountry to the west. The valley is Cow Creek Umpqua ancestral territory, and the creek that gives it its name flows through town on its way to join the South Umpqua River.

The [Cow Creek Back Country Byway](https://www.blm.gov/sites/default/files/documents/files/rbg_cowcreek_brochure.pdf) — the same 45-mile BLM scenic drive that begins at Glendale — terminates near Riddle, making the town the northern bookend of the byway. The drive through the canyon follows the creek, the railroad, and the remnants of 19th-century gold mining country.

Riddle itself is a town of roughly 1,200 people. The economy that built it — nickel mining and timber — is largely gone. What remains is a quiet Cow Creek Valley community with a fascinating industrial history and a mountain where, if you know what you're looking for, you can still pull nickel ore out of the ground with your bare hands.

## Getting to Riddle

**I-5 Exit 103** connects to Highway 39, which leads west to Riddle. The town center is approximately 3 miles from the interchange.

Riddle is 18 miles south of Roseburg, 35 miles north of Grants Pass, and approximately 195 miles south of Portland on I-5. Nickel Mountain is accessible via Nickel Mountain Road, approximately 4 miles west of town.
