## Where the River Is the Town

The city of Rogue River, Oregon, did not choose a subtle name.

It sits where Evans Creek meets the Rogue River, at I-5 Exit 48, in a valley notch between the Siskiyou Mountains to the south and the foothills of the Cascades to the east. The town is 2,300 people, less than one square mile, and almost entirely defined by the water running through it.

The [Rogue River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogue_River_(Oregon)) is one of the original eight rivers designated under the 1968 National Wild and Scenic Rivers Act. It drops from the Cascade Range near Crater Lake, runs 215 miles through some of the most rugged canyon country in the Pacific Northwest, and empties into the ocean at Gold Beach. The section downstream of this town — the Wild and Scenic stretch through the Rogue River canyon — is one of the most sought-after multi-day rafting experiences in the western United States. Permit holders line up months in advance for the chance to float it.

The city of Rogue River is where the accessible river and the wild river begin to diverge. Upstream, the Rogue is valley water — wide, warm in summer, bordered by vineyards and orchards. Downstream, it enters the canyon. The character changes. The current quickens. The banks rise.

This town sits at the hinge.

## Fishing Capital of the Rogue Valley

If there is a single activity that defines the city of Rogue River, it's fishing.

The Rogue is one of Oregon's premier salmon and steelhead rivers, and the stretch near this town offers some of the most accessible and productive fishing water in the system. Spring Chinook, fall Chinook, summer and winter steelhead, and half-pounders (juvenile steelhead that return to the river early) all run through here at different times of year.

Guided drift boat trips are the most popular way to fish the Rogue. Outfitters like [Drift Adventures](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attractions-g52044-Activities-Rogue_River_Oregon.html), [King of the Rogue Outfitters](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attractions-g52044-Activities-Rogue_River_Oregon.html), and [Briggs Rogue River Trips](https://www.briggsroguerivertrips.com/rogue-river-fishing/wild-scenic) launch from access points near town, offering everything from half-day floats to multi-day wilderness fishing expeditions through the Wild and Scenic canyon.

The [Rogue River Chamber of Commerce](https://www.rogueriverchamber.com/about-rogue-river) positions the town as "central to many outdoor activities" — and while that's true for skiing, biking, and hiking as well, fishing is the engine. It's what brings visitors, what sustains the outfitter economy, and what gives the town its identity beyond the interstate exit.

## Palmerton's Trees

The most unexpected thing in Rogue River is a five-acre arboretum planted by a Spanish-American War veteran.

[Palmerton Arboretum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmerton_Arboretum) sits on West Evans Creek Road, a five-minute drive from downtown. Orin Palmerton, the veteran, purchased the land in the 1920s and began planting domestic and exotic trees from around the world — a personal nursery that grew into a collection of nearly 90 species, from Monkey Puzzle Trees to Norwegian Spruce.

In 1966, [Palmerton sold the land to the city](https://soredi.org/community-gems-palmerton-park/), hoping his tree nursery would be preserved as a park. Today, it is. Evans Creek runs through the property, and the arboretum is open to the public year-round, free of charge. [Jefferson Public Radio](https://www.ijpr.org/show/as-it-was/2014-10-24/orin-palmerton-sells-his-arboretum-to-preserve) documented the arboretum's history as part of its "As It Was" series — a story about one person's decades-long commitment to something beautiful, and a town's decision to honor it.

For a town of 2,300 people, Palmerton Arboretum is an unusual civic asset. It's quiet, well-maintained, and exactly the kind of place you wouldn't know about unless someone told you.

## A River Town Economy

Rogue River's economy is small, seasonal, and honest about its constraints.

The median household income is approximately $54,537, with a poverty rate of 12.59%, according to [World Population Review](https://worldpopulationreview.com/us-cities/oregon/rogue-river). The median age is 50 — one of the older median ages in the Rogue Valley, reflecting a community with a significant retiree population. The population has been declining slowly, down roughly 5% since the 2020 Census.

Most working residents commute to Grants Pass (8 miles west) or Medford (20 miles southeast). The local economy is driven by outdoor recreation — fishing guides, rafting outfitters, small motels, and the services that support river tourism. [Travel Oregon](https://traveloregon.com/places-to-go/cities/rogue-river/) highlights the town's community restaurants, RV-friendly campgrounds, and mountain biking access alongside the river activities.

This is not a town with a diversified economic base. It's a town built around a river, and the river sustains it — directly through tourism and outfitting, indirectly through the quality of life that keeps retirees and remote workers choosing to live here despite the limited local services.

## Housing on the Rogue

Rogue River's housing market is small and variable.

The average home value is approximately $463,000 as of early 2026, according to [Zillow](https://www.zillow.com/home-values/36954/rogue-river-or/) — up about 2.4% year-over-year. The median sale price tracked by [Redfin](https://www.redfin.com/city/16265/OR/Rogue-River/housing-market) is lower, around $337,000, reflecting the gap between the listing prices of river-view properties and what actually transacts in a low-volume market. With only a handful of homes selling per month, individual transactions can swing the numbers significantly.

The town's appeal is lifestyle over investment. Riverfront and river-view properties command premiums, while more modest homes on the hillsides and along Evans Creek offer entry points that are still accessible relative to the rest of the Rogue Valley. For someone who wants to live on or near the Rogue River without paying Grants Pass or Ashland prices, Rogue River is one of the few options left.

## Getting to Rogue River

**I-5 Exit 48** drops directly into the city of Rogue River. The town straddles the old Pacific Highway (Depot Street / Main Street), with the Rogue River running just to the south.

Rogue River is 8 miles east of Grants Pass, 20 miles northwest of Medford, and approximately 250 miles south of Portland on I-5. [Rogue Valley International-Medford Airport (MFR)](https://www.jacksoncountyor.gov/airport) is 22 miles southeast.
