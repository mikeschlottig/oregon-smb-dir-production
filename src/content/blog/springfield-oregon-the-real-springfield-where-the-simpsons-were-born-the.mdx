## The Other Half of the Valley's Twin Cities

For decades, Springfield has lived in Eugene's shadow. The university town to the west got the cultural cachet, the food scene features, and the national attention. Springfield got the timber mills, the working-class reputation, and the quiet competence of a city that keeps the lights on while its neighbor gets the magazine covers.

That's changing.

In January 2026, the Register-Guard reported that United Van Lines' 49th Annual Movers Study ranked Oregon the #1 state to move to in 2025, with Eugene-Springfield specifically named as a top metro destination — 85% of all inbound movers. The study highlighted Springfield's lower cost of living compared to other Oregon cities, combined with its proximity to Eugene and Portland, as major draws.

Springfield is not becoming Eugene. It's becoming the more affordable, more practical, increasingly attractive version of itself — a city of 61,200 that has its own identity, its own river, and its own quietly remarkable history.

## Yes, It's *That* Springfield

In 2012, Smithsonian Magazine published the interview where Matt Groening finally confirmed what Oregonians had long suspected: the Springfield in *The Simpsons* is named after Springfield, Oregon.

Groening grew up in Portland, two hours north, with parents named Homer and Margaret (Marge), sisters named Lisa and Maggie, and brothers who inspired Bart (an anagram of "brat"). Portland street names — Flanders, Quimby, Kearney, Lovejoy — became character names. But the city itself? Springfield, Oregon.

Eugene, Cascades & Coast maintains an unofficial Simpsons Tour of Springfield, noting murals, landmarks, and locations that echo the show. The Simpsons connection is playful, widely embraced, and the kind of cultural footnote that a city can either cringe at or lean into. Springfield leans in.

When asked why he chose the name, Groening told Smithsonian: "I also figured out that Springfield was one of the most common city names in the U.S. In anticipation of the success of the show, I thought, 'This will be cool; everyone will think it's their Springfield.' And they do."

## The McKenzie River City

If Eugene claims the Willamette, Springfield claims the McKenzie.

The McKenzie River enters Springfield from the east, flowing out of the Cascade Range through some of the clearest water in Oregon. The river's headwaters at Clear Lake are fed by 3,000-year-old springs, and by the time the McKenzie reaches Springfield, it's still remarkably clean — the city's drinking water source and one of the finest recreational rivers in the Pacific Northwest.

The McKenzie corridor offers world-class fly fishing, whitewater rafting, and kayaking upstream, with calmer stretches for floating and swimming as it approaches town. Springfield's Island Park along the Willamette provides urban green space at the confluence of the McKenzie and Willamette rivers.

## Timber, Transition, and What Comes Next

Springfield's economy was built on timber. Weyerhaeuser operated a major plywood mill in Springfield for decades before selling it in 2007, and the company still maintains a TOPS plant on N. 42nd Street. At its peak, Weyerhaeuser employed approximately 4,500 people across Oregon.

The timber industry's contraction hit Springfield harder than Eugene, where the university provided an economic cushion. Springfield's recovery has been slower, steadier, and more blue-collar — built on manufacturing, healthcare (PeaceHealth's Sacred Heart Medical Center is the region's largest hospital), and the growing appeal of affordable housing in a metro area where Eugene's prices have climbed steadily.

The Dorris Ranch Living History Filbert Farm — the oldest commercial filbert (hazelnut) orchard in the United States — sits along the Middle Fork of the Willamette in Springfield, offering trails, seasonal events, and a quiet connection to the agricultural roots that preceded the timber era.

## The Numbers

Springfield's population is approximately 61,200, per the U.S. Census Bureau, making it the ninth-most populous city in Oregon and the second-largest in the Eugene-Springfield metro. The city's diversity, while still limited (78% White), is growing, and the population has been stable to slightly declining (-1.1% since 2020) — a trend the United Van Lines data suggests may be reversing.

## Getting to Springfield

**I-5 Exits 191, 194, and 195** provide access to Springfield. The city is separated from Eugene primarily by I-5 and the Willamette River. Highway 126 (McKenzie Highway) heads east from Springfield into the McKenzie River corridor and the Cascade Range.

Springfield is directly adjacent to Eugene, 110 miles south of Portland, and 70 miles north of Roseburg on I-5.
