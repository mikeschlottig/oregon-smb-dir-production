## The Town That Didn't Shrink

Sutherlin has a population of 8,700 people, and the remarkable thing about that number is how much larger it is than it used to be.

In 1940, the town had 525 residents. The timber boom of the 1940s and 1950s changed everything — sawmills opened along Central Avenue and the railroad corridor, and by 1950 the population had quadrupled to 2,230. Mills like L&H Lumber, Springfield Plywood, Nordic Plywood, and Murphy Plywood operated in succession over the following decades, each one employing hundreds.

Then the mills closed. Most of them, anyway. The same federal timber policy shifts that devastated Roseburg and the rest of Douglas County hit Sutherlin hard. But unlike many Oregon timber towns that peaked in the 1970s and have been declining since, Sutherlin kept growing. The 2020 Census recorded 8,524 residents — up 9% from 2010 — and [World Population Review](https://worldpopulationreview.com/us-cities/oregon/sutherlin) shows continued growth at about 0.46% annually.

The reason is partly geographic. Sutherlin sits 12 miles north of Roseburg on I-5, close enough to benefit from the county seat's services and employment while offering lower housing costs and a quieter residential character. It has become, functionally, a bedroom community for Roseburg — but one with its own identity, its own schools, and its own Central Avenue commercial strip.

## What's Here

Sutherlin's Central Avenue runs parallel to I-5 and serves as the town's main street — gas stations, restaurants, a grocery store, and the kind of practical small-town commercial corridor that keeps a community of 8,700 functioning without a trip to Roseburg.

The town sits at the junction of I-5 and the western segment of OR-138, which leads northwest through the Coast Range to Elkton and eventually connects to OR-38 toward the Pacific coast at Reedsport. For travelers headed from the Umpqua Valley to the southern Oregon coast, Sutherlin is the natural departure point.

The median household income is approximately $77,183, with a poverty rate of 13% and a median age of 42.5, per [World Population Review](https://worldpopulationreview.com/us-cities/oregon/sutherlin). Nearly 28% of residents are 65 or older — one of the higher senior populations in Douglas County, reflecting the retirement migration that has characterized the Umpqua Valley for decades.

## Getting to Sutherlin

**I-5 Exit 136** provides access to Sutherlin via Central Avenue. OR-138 west departs from Sutherlin toward Elkton and the coast.

Sutherlin is 12 miles north of Roseburg, 58 miles south of Eugene, and approximately 168 miles south of Portland on I-5.
