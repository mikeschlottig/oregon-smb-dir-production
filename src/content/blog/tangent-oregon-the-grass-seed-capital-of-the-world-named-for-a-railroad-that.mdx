## The Grass Seed Capital of the World

Tangent has two claims to fame, and both of them are perfectly, wonderfully Tangent.

The first is its name. Like its neighbor Halsey to the south, Tangent was a station on the Southern Pacific line through the Willamette Valley. The station sat along a stretch of track that ran straight — without a single curve — for more than 20 miles. In geometric terms, a tangent is a straight line. The railroad named the station for the track's geometry, and the town kept the name.

The second claim is agricultural. Tangent calls itself the **"Grass Seed Capital of the World,"** and while that might sound like small-town boosterism, the Willamette Valley actually is the global center of grass seed production. The valley's mild, wet winters and dry summers create ideal conditions for growing ryegrass, fescue, and bentgrass seed. Linn County is the heart of this industry, and Tangent — sitting squarely in the middle of it — has embraced the title.

The town's proximity to OR-34 and I-5 makes it a logistics hub for agricultural businesses. Tractor Supply, seed companies, and agriculture-related industries cluster in and around Tangent, drawn by affordable land, good highway access, and proximity to both Albany (7 miles north) and Corvallis (9 miles west, home of Oregon State University's agricultural programs).

## Small Town, Big Personality

At 1,231 people, Tangent is the kind of town where the stories are better than the statistics.

The city has a Mayor Kitty Cat story — local legend involving a feline that allegedly held unofficial municipal office. The historic City Hall, originally the town's first post office, still stands along Highway 99E, and the original boardwalk in front of it has been preserved. Pioneer Park and the David Brush Recreational Trail offer green space and walking paths.

Two food trucks and one restaurant saloon operate along Highway 99E, which runs through the middle of town. That's not a limitation — it's a ratio. Three food options for 1,231 people is roughly one establishment per 410 residents, which is a better ratio than most cities can claim.

Tangent is a home rule city, meaning it operates under its own charter rather than state default rules — an unusual level of self-governance for a town this size, reflecting a community that takes its independence seriously.

## Getting to Tangent

**I-5 Exit 228** (OR-34, Lebanon/Corvallis) provides access to Tangent. The town is located along Highway 99E, approximately 2 miles south of the interchange.

Tangent is 7 miles south of Albany, 9 miles east of Corvallis, and approximately 75 miles south of Portland on I-5.
