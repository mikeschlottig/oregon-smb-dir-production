## The Suburb That Earned City Status

Tigard has a problem that most Portland-area suburbs would love to have: it's too successful to be called a suburb.

With approximately 58,434 people, Tigard is the 10th largest city in Oregon — larger than Corvallis, larger than Albany, larger than Grants Pass. It's home to 3,400 businesses employing around 45,000 workers from around the region. It has direct freeway access via both I-5 and OR-217. It has Washington Square, the Portland metro's premier shopping center. And it has something that most cities its size in the metro area don't: 550 acres of parks and over 16 miles of trails.

The Fanno Creek Trail system is Tigard's signature. The 30-plus-acre Fanno Creek Park stretches through the heart of the city, offering boardwalks, soft and hard trails, and bridges that traverse the creek through grassy and wooded wetlands. Cook Family Park on the Tualatin River adds play structures, picnic shelters, soccer fields, and the Ki-a-Kuts Bike and Pedestrian Bridge connecting to Tualatin's Community Park.

Tigard's growth has been steady and substantial — 0.98% annually, per World Population Review, with over 3,400 new residents since 2020. The **median household income is $108,823** with a poverty rate of just 6.74%. The median age is 39.4.

## The Economic Engine

Tigard's economic profile is diversified in a way that single-industry suburbs aren't. Greater Portland Inc describes the city as having "a diverse economy, strong schools, outstanding parks and direct access to two major freeways" — the combination that has made it one of the most desirable and livable cities in Oregon.

Washington Square, located in the northeast portion of the city, anchors the retail economy with major national retailers, restaurants, and entertainment. But Tigard's employment base extends well beyond retail into professional services, technology, healthcare, and light manufacturing — the kind of economic mix that sustains a city through retail downturns and tech cycles.

The Broadway Rose Theatre, the Portland metro's only professional musical theatre company dedicated exclusively to musical theatre, adds a cultural dimension that most suburbs lack.

## Getting to Tigard

**I-5 Exits 291 and 292** provide access to Tigard. Exit 292 connects to OR-99W, the primary commercial corridor through the city. OR-217 runs north-south through Tigard, connecting to Beaverton and US-26.

Tigard is 10 miles southwest of downtown Portland, directly adjacent to Tualatin, Lake Oswego, and Beaverton.
