## Where Silicon Forest Meets the Suburbs

Tualatin is a semiconductor town that doesn't look like one.

As one of the largest employment hubs for semiconductor manufacturing in Oregon, Tualatin sits at the southern edge of the "Silicon Forest" — the Portland metro's tech corridor that includes Intel, Lam Research, and dozens of advanced manufacturing firms. The city is a regional leader in advanced manufacturing and information technology, drawing on Washington County's concentration of engineering talent and the infrastructure that I-5 and Highway 99W provide.

But drive through Tualatin and you won't see smokestacks or industrial parks lining the highway. You'll see Bridgeport Village — one of the nation's premier outdoor lifestyle centers — with over 75 retailers, boutiques, restaurants, and a Regal IMAX theater. You'll see the Tualatin River winding through parks and green spaces. You'll see a suburban city that has managed to combine high-wage employment with genuine livability.

## The Numbers

Tualatin's population is approximately 28,112, per World Population Review, growing modestly at 0.14% annually. The city's explosive growth phase (from 22,911 in 2000 to 27,884 in 2020) has leveled off, but the economic fundamentals remain strong.

The **median household income is $104,043**, per World Population Review (Greater Portland Inc reports the ESRI estimate at $109,644), with a poverty rate of 9.99%. The median age is 37.4. The city's diversity is notable: 23.4% Hispanic/Latino, 16.4% two or more races — significantly more diverse than the Oregon average.

Tualatin straddles Washington and Clackamas counties, giving it access to two of the most economically productive counties in Oregon. The city's housing stock ranges from starter homes to executive properties, and the school options span two high-performing districts.

## The Tualatin River

The city takes its name from the Tualatin River, which flows through the community on its way to the Willamette. The Tualatin River National Wildlife Refuge, managed by the U.S. Fish & Wildlife Service, provides 1,856 acres of wetlands, forests, and grasslands within the metro area — a natural amenity that gives Tualatin a green identity distinct from its commercial and industrial character.

Tualatin Commons, the city's public gathering space, hosts events, a farmers' market, and community activities throughout the year.

## Getting to Tualatin

**I-5 Exit 289** provides access to Tualatin via Nyberg Street and Lower Boones Ferry Road. Highway 99W also connects Tualatin to Tigard, Sherwood, and the wine country to the southwest.

Tualatin is 12 miles south of downtown Portland, directly adjacent to Tigard and Lake Oswego, and 20 miles north of Wilsonville on I-5.
