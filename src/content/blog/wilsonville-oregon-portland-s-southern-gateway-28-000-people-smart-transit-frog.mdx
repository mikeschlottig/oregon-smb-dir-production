## The Last Exit Before Portland

Wilsonville is where the Willamette Valley ends and the Portland metro begins.

At I-5 Exit 283, southbound travelers leave the suburban sprawl and enter the agricultural flatland. Northbound travelers see the first signs of metro-density development — office parks, apartment complexes, and the infrastructure of a city that has positioned itself as Portland's most livable southern suburb.

Wilsonville's population is approximately 28,481, per World Population Review, growing at 1.04% annually. The city nearly doubled between 2000 and 2020 (from 14,068 to 26,723) and was one of the fastest-growing cities in Oregon during the 2010s. Growth has moderated recently — the Wilsonville Spokesman reported just 174 new residents between 2024 and 2025 — but the **Frog Pond** area, a major single-family and multifamily development on the city's eastern edge, is setting the stage for the next wave of in-migration.

## SMART Transit: Free Rides

Wilsonville operates **SMART** (South Metro Area Regional Transit), one of the few fare-free public transit systems in Oregon. SMART buses connect Wilsonville to Salem, Canby, and the Portland metro's WES commuter rail station at the Wilsonville Transit Center. In collaboration with ODOT, SMART buses can use highway shoulders on I-205 when traffic drops below 35 mph — a "Bus on Shoulder" program that prioritizes transit reliability during congestion.

Free public transit in a suburb of 28,000 people is unusual. It reflects Wilsonville's corporate tax base — the city hosts significant employers in technology, manufacturing, and professional services — and a civic philosophy that treats transit as infrastructure rather than charity.

## The Corporate Suburb

Wilsonville's economy is anchored by technology and corporate employers. The city's location — equidistant between Portland and Salem, with easy I-5 access and a skilled workforce — has attracted companies that need space, talent, and connectivity without downtown Portland costs.

The **median household income is $96,236**, per World Population Review, with a poverty rate of 8.79%. The median age is 37.7. These numbers describe a professional-class suburb with a mix of young families, tech workers, and established homeowners.

## Getting to Wilsonville

**I-5 Exits 283 and 286** provide access to Wilsonville. Exit 283 leads to central Wilsonville and the Town Center area. Exit 286 (Stafford/N. Wilsonville) accesses the northern portions of the city.

Wilsonville is 20 miles south of Portland, 40 miles north of Salem, and serves as the transition point between the Willamette Valley and the Portland metro on I-5.
