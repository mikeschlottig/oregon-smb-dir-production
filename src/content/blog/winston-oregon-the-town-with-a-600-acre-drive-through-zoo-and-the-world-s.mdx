## The Zoo You Drive Through

Somewhere in Winston, Oregon — a town of 5,500 people just south of Roseburg — there are cheetahs.

Not at a sanctuary you need an appointment to visit. Not behind a fence at the edge of a national park. At a [drive-through zoo](https://wildlifesafari.net/) where you steer your own car along a 4.5-mile loop through 600 acres of open-range habitat, and the animals — giraffes, zebras, ostriches, lions, bears, bison, elk, and yes, cheetahs — are right there. Some of them will walk up to your car window.

[Wildlife Safari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_Safari) opened in 1972, founded by Frank Hart with a mission to create a place dedicated to saving and protecting endangered species. Today it is an AZA-accredited (Association of Zoos and Aquariums) facility that is home to over 600 animals representing more than 100 species from Africa, Asia, and the Americas. Annual attendance exceeds 200,000 visitors.

The headline statistic: Wildlife Safari operates the [second-most successful cheetah breeding program in the world](https://toandfrofam.com/wildlife-safari-winston-oregon/). Since the program launched in 1972, more than 240 cheetahs have been born at the park — a contribution to species survival that would be remarkable at any zoo, let alone one located off Exit 113 in rural Douglas County.

The park was nominated for [USA TODAY's 10Best Readers' Choice Award](https://10best.usatoday.com/awards/wildlife-safari-winston-oregon/) for Best Safari Park in 2026 — competing against drive-through wildlife parks across the country.

Beyond the drive-through loop, the always-free [Safari Village](https://wildlifesafari.net/pages/safari-village) offers a walk-through experience with smaller animals, camel rides, a train ride, and an aviary. Close-up Animal Enrichment Encounters allow visitors to interact directly with specific species for an additional fee.

## The Town Behind the Safari

Winston exists, for most visitors, as the town where Wildlife Safari is. That's not entirely fair — but it's not entirely wrong either.

The town sits along the South Umpqua River, 7 miles south of Roseburg on I-5. It has a small commercial core, residential neighborhoods, schools, and the kind of rural Southern Oregon character that defines the Umpqua Valley communities. The economy is anchored by the safari park, local agriculture, and proximity to Roseburg's services and employment.

Winston is also at the edge of the [Umpqua Valley wine region](https://www.umpquavalleywineries.org/wine-facts/) — over 30 wineries and 70 vineyards are scattered across the surrounding hills, making the area a growing destination for wine tourism. The combination of a world-class wildlife park and an emerging wine region gives Winston more visitor appeal than its population and interstate profile would suggest.

## Getting to Winston

**I-5 Exit 113** provides access to Winston. Wildlife Safari is located at 1790 Safari Road — follow signs from the exit.

Winston is 7 miles south of Roseburg, 30 miles north of Canyonville, and approximately 178 miles south of Portland on I-5. The park is open daily 9:00 AM to 6:00 PM year-round (closed Thanksgiving and Christmas).
