## The Inn That Wouldn't Close

In 1883, a local merchant named Henry Smith built a travelers' inn at the Wolf Creek stagecoach stop in southern Oregon's Siskiyou Mountains. It was a 16-day journey from San Francisco to Portland in those days, and the inn at Wolf Creek — roughly the midpoint through the most rugged canyon section — was not a luxury. It was a necessity.

The railroad came through four years later, in 1887, and most stagecoach stops along the route died. Wolf Creek's didn't. The inn transitioned from serving stage passengers to railroad travelers, then to automobile tourists when the Pacific Highway replaced the rails as the primary route north. Then Interstate 5 bypassed the highway, and most of the small towns along the old road faded further.

Wolf Creek's inn still didn't close.

The [Wolf Creek Inn State Heritage Site](https://stateparks.oregon.gov/index.cfm?do=park.profile&parkId=74) is, by the State of Oregon's count, the oldest continuously operated hotel in the Pacific Northwest. It has been open — in some form, under some name — for over 140 years. Oregon Parks and Recreation took ownership in 1975 and restored the building to its period character. Today it operates as a country inn with overnight rooms and a restaurant, managed under state stewardship.

The guest register reads like a century of American cultural history. Jack London reportedly completed his novel *The Valley of the Moon* while staying at the inn. Clark Gable was a regular in the 1930s, stopping by on fishing trips to the Rogue River. Carole Lombard and Orson Welles signed the book. [Travel Oregon reports](https://traveloregon.com/things-to-do/culture-history/historic-sites-oregon-trail/grants-getaways-wolf-creek-inn/) that Robert Redford stayed here while filming fly-fishing scenes for *A River Runs Through It*.

And then there are the ghosts.

## Room Eight

The Wolf Creek Inn is, depending on who you ask, one of the most actively haunted buildings in Oregon.

The Travel Channel's *Ghost Adventures* crew investigated the property. Paranormal experts from Nevada's Pioneer Saloon visited and reported "something really heavy going on throughout the building." Room eight gets the most reports — unexplained sounds, moving objects, the usual catalog of things that would be terrifying in any building but somehow feel appropriate in one that's been hosting strangers for 140 years.

[Travel Oregon's haunted feature](https://traveloregon.com/things-to-do/trip-ideas/favorite-trips/haunted-tales-wolf-creek-inn/) notes that the consensus among visitors and investigators is that the activity is "friendly acts of mischief" rather than anything sinister. The inn leans into its spooky reputation during Halloween season with full decor and themed events.

Whether you believe in ghosts or not, sleeping in a room where Jack London wrote and Clark Gable napped carries its own kind of atmosphere.

## The Town Around the Inn

Wolf Creek itself is a community of roughly 200 people — a cluster of homes, a few local merchants, a state park, and the inn that gives the place its identity. The surrounding landscape is steep, forested canyon country in the transition zone between the Rogue Valley and the Umpqua Valley to the north.

[London Peak](https://www.southernoregon.com/profiles/wolfcreek/index.html), named for Jack London's visits, offers an ADA-approved old-growth hiking trail — described as Southern Oregon's most accessible old-growth experience — with a viewpoint above the Wolf Creek valley. Wolf Creek Park provides day-use picnic facilities and access to the surrounding forest.

The [Cow Creek Back Country Byway](https://www.blm.gov/sites/default/files/documents/files/rbg_cowcreek_brochure.pdf), a 45-mile scenic drive managed by the BLM, connects the towns of Riddle and Glendale through historic gold mining country and follows the active railroad grade originally built in 1869. The byway is accessible from the Wolf Creek area and offers a dramatic alternative to I-5 for travelers willing to trade speed for scenery.

## Getting to Wolf Creek

**I-5 Exit 76** drops directly into Wolf Creek. The Wolf Creek Inn is visible from the exit — 100 Front Street, less than a minute from the interstate.

Wolf Creek is 20 miles north of Grants Pass, 50 miles northwest of Medford, and approximately 230 miles south of Portland on I-5.
