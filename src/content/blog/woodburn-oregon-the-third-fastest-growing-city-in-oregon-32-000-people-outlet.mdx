## Oregon's Fastest-Growing Story

In July 2025, the Statesman Journal reported that Woodburn was the third-fastest growing city in Oregon among those with populations over 10,000. The numbers back the headline: Woodburn's population is approximately 32,600, per World Population Review, growing at **3.47% annually** — a rate that has added over 6,500 people since the 2020 census, a 25% increase in six years.

Woodburn is not growing because it's trendy. It's growing because it's practical.

The city sits at I-5 Exit 271, 30 miles south of Portland and 18 miles north of Salem — equidistant between the state's two largest employment centers. Housing is significantly more affordable than Portland, Tigard, or Wilsonville. The commute to Portland is manageable. And the community itself offers something that most I-5 corridor towns don't: genuine cultural identity.

## Oregon's Latino Capital

Woodburn is one of the most culturally diverse cities in Oregon. The Hispanic/Latino population is estimated at over 60%, making Woodburn the most predominantly Latino city in the state. This isn't a recent phenomenon — Woodburn's Latino community has roots in the agricultural labor force that has worked the Willamette Valley's farms, nurseries, and food processing plants for generations.

The result is a city where the taquería is not a trend but a tradition, where Spanish is heard as frequently as English on the streets, and where the cultural calendar includes Fiesta Mexicana alongside the Fourth of July. Woodburn's restaurants — particularly its Mexican, Salvadoran, and Guatemalan spots — are a genuine culinary draw, offering food that Portland foodies drive south to find.

The median household income is $67,347, per World Population Review, with a poverty rate of 16.32%. These numbers reflect the economic reality of a working-class community where agricultural and manufacturing wages are the norm. But Woodburn's growth suggests that affordability and community are winning the argument against higher wages elsewhere.

## Tulips and Outlets

Two institutions draw visitors to Woodburn from across the region.

The **Wooden Shoe Tulip Festival**, held at the Wooden Shoe Tulip Farm east of town, runs from mid-March through late April and is one of Oregon's signature spring events. Now in its 42nd year, the festival features 40 acres of blooming tulips, hot air balloons, wine tastings, a specialty gift shop, food and craft markets, and children's activities. It's the kind of event that fills Instagram feeds and Highway 211 with equal enthusiasm.

The **Woodburn Premium Outlets**, directly off I-5 at Exit 271, is one of the largest outlet shopping centers in Oregon. It draws shoppers from Portland, Salem, and beyond — and provides a significant portion of Woodburn's sales tax revenue (Oregon has no sales tax, but the economic activity is real).

## Getting to Woodburn

**I-5 Exit 271** provides direct access to Woodburn via OR-214. The Woodburn Premium Outlets are immediately adjacent to the interchange.

Woodburn is 30 miles south of Portland, 18 miles north of Salem, and centrally located on the I-5 corridor between Oregon's two largest cities.
