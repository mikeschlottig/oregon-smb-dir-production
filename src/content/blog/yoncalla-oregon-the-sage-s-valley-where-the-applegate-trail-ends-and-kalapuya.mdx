## A Name That Carries Two Histories

Yoncalla is named for the [Yoncalla band of the Kalapuya people](https://www.reddit.com/r/oregon/comments/158k2i0/istsyilshe_of_the_yoncalla_band_of_the_kalapuya/) — the Indigenous inhabitants who lived in this section of the Umpqua Valley since time immemorial. The word itself is Kalapuyan, and the band's territory encompassed the valley and its tributaries in what is now northern Douglas County.

The second history arrived in 1849, when Jesse Applegate — one of the most influential figures in early Oregon — settled on a land claim in the valley and named his new home after the local tribe. Applegate had helped blaze the [Applegate Trail](https://www.nps.gov/cali/learn/historyculture/upload/Applegate-Trail_final_sm.pdf) in 1846, an alternative southern route into Oregon designed to avoid the deadly Columbia River rapids that had killed members of his own family, including his son, during the 1843 crossing.

Applegate farmed, raised cattle, maintained a large personal library, and became known as the "[Sage of Yoncalla](https://archiveswest.orbiscascade.org/ark:80444/xv12374)" — a title given by friends who valued his writing and his influence over Oregon public affairs. The [Applegate Century Ranch](https://www.oregonencyclopedia.org/articles/applegate-farm-charles-applegate/) on nearby Elk Creek, established by his sons in the 1860s, became one of Douglas County's enduring agricultural landmarks.

The Kalapuya story is older and less documented. Some Yoncalla Kalapuya families managed to remain in the area after the forced removals and reservation relocations of the 1850s — a rare exception in western Oregon, possibly aided by relationships with the powerful Applegate clan. Their presence in the valley persisted into the 20th century, a quiet thread of continuity beneath the settler narrative.

## A Small Town in the Pass

Today, Yoncalla is a city of roughly 1,040 people in the hills between Roseburg and Drain. The median household income is $55,260, with a poverty rate of 13.39% and a median age of 46.9, per [World Population Review](https://worldpopulationreview.com/us-cities/oregon/yoncalla). It's a small, aging community with a stable population that has hovered around 1,000 for decades.

The surrounding Scotts Valley and Elk Creek country offer the kind of rolling, oak-dotted landscape that drew settlers in the 1850s and still defines the rural character of northern Douglas County. Yoncalla sits at the transition between the Umpqua Valley to the south and the Calapooya Mountains to the north — the same geographic divide that the Applegate Trail was built to cross.

## Getting to Yoncalla

**I-5 Exit 150** provides access to Yoncalla. The town center is less than a mile from the interchange.

Yoncalla is 28 miles north of Roseburg, 45 miles south of Eugene, and approximately 152 miles south of Portland on I-5.
